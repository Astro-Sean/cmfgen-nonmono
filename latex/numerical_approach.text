We model the double-explosion scenario with a two-stage pipeline coupling the multi-group radiation hydrodynamics code STELLA (Blinnikov et al. 1998, 2006) with the non-LTE radiative transfer code CMFGEN (Hillier & Miller 1998; Dessart et al. 2015). In the first stage (SN_A), a Type IIb progenitor - the solar-metallicity 12 Msun model s12.0 of Woosley & Heger (2007), stripped to a ~3 Msun helium core and matched to model 12C of Ergon et al. (2022) for SN 2011dh - is exploded with a thermal bomb of 0.9 x 10^51 erg deposited over 10 s in the inner 0.3 Msun, yielding a final kinetic energy of ~0.7 foe. A thin hydrogen envelope of 0.03-0.10 Msun (X = 0.7) is added to the surface, and a low-density (Mdot = 10^-6 Msun yr^-1) sigmoid-blended circumstellar wind extends to ~10^17 cm for numerical stability. A 56Ni mass of 0.075 Msun is distributed over the inner 0.3 Msun. STELLA evolves the explosion with 500 Lagrangian zones using a GEAR integrator (epsilon = 3 x 10^-3), with E_KO = 0 to preserve the pre-SN velocity structure, FitTau = 5 for frequency-dependent opacity, and a minimum of N_zon/10 diffusion zones to prevent late-time N_cnd collapse. In the second stage (SN_B), the SN_A ejecta is homologously evolved to ~400 d and serves as the CSM. A H/He shell (1.0 Msun, X = 0.7, Y = 0.3) with a power-law density profile (rho ~ r^-8) following Dessart et al. (2016) is placed at the centre and launched at 5000 km s^-1 into the CSM. STELLA evolves the shell-CSM interaction for 45 d with E_KO = 0 (preserving both shell and CSM velocities), enhanced artificial viscosity (A_Q = 10) for the strong velocity reversal, and no 56Ni (the luminosity is entirely interaction-powered). At selected epochs (30 d post-SBO for SN_A; 1 d and 35 d post-launch for SN_B), snapshots are extracted from the STELLA output and remapped onto CMFGEN depth grids (N_D ~ 155) using a tau-based adaptive algorithm that resolves ionisation fronts and velocity reversals. CMFGEN computes non-LTE level populations and emergent spectra with VEL_LAW = 11 (reading the non-homologous velocity directly). The non-monotonic velocity field - containing shocks, velocity reversals, and compression zones where the comoving-frame frequency gradient Gamma becomes negative - is treated with the Knop et al. (2009) formal solver: the generalised opacity is kept strictly positive by using |Gamma| in place of Gamma, while the signed Gamma is retained in the explicit source term, and the upwind frequency direction is chosen sign-dependently (previous frequency for expanding flow, next frequency for compressing flow) with a Gauss-Seidel iteration over great iterations to handle the not-yet-computed next-frequency intensities. The moment-equation solver is bypassed entirely (USE_LAM_ES = T) and J_nu is computed directly from the formal solution with a tridiagonal depth-coupling Eddington-factor solver (NUM_BNDS = 3, N_TYPE = N_ON_J). The temperature, electron density, and impurity populations are fixed to the hydrodynamic values (FIX_T = T, FIX_NE = T, FIX_IMP = T) throughout, as appropriate for a static snapshot. The iteration begins with pure Lambda iterations from LTE initial populations (LTE_EST = T) with MAX_LAM = 10^5, followed by automatic transition to full BA (linearisation) iterations when the maximum population correction falls below 50% (DO_LAM_AUTO = T); Ng acceleration (DO_NG = T, IBEG_NG = 50, BW_NG = 60) is applied during the Lambda phase. The inner boundary uses a diffusion condition seeded by the STELLA luminosity. The complete pipeline - progenitor conversion, STELLA evolution, snapshot extraction, CMFGEN spectral synthesis, and dependency-chained job submission - is orchestrated by a single workflow manager on the MPCDF Viper HPC cluster.


The Knop et al. (2009) Non-Monotonic Formal Solver

The interaction-powered SN_B model produces ejecta in which the radial velocity
field is non-monotonic: a fast H/He shell (5000 km/s) ploughs into the slower
SN_A CSM (~1000-3000 km/s), driving a forward shock into the CSM and a reverse
shock back into the shell.  Between the two shocks the velocity decreases
outward (dV/dR < 0) over a broad compression zone, so the velocity-gradient
parameter sigma = dln V/dln R - 1 drops well below -1 (reaching sigma ~ -166
on the finest grid).  This violates the fundamental assumption on which
CMFGEN's standard comoving-frame (CMF) formal solver is built: that the
outflow velocity increases monotonically outward, so each observer-frame
frequency resonates with exactly one comoving frequency at one depth (the
Sobolev single-resonance picture).  When sigma < -1 the CMF frequency
coupling reverses sign and the standard solver becomes numerically unstable.
We therefore replace the standard formal solver with the non-monotonic
formal solver of Knop, Hauschildt & Baron (2009, A&A 493, 1197), implemented
in the routine FG_J_CMF_NONMONO_V1.  The solver modifies only how the mean
intensity J_nu is computed in the formal solution; the rest of the CMFGEN
iteration cycle (statistical equilibrium, temperature correction, Ng
acceleration, Sobolev escape probabilities, line opacities, atomic data) is
unchanged.  Below we describe the mathematical structure of the solver, the
practical iteration scheme, and an analogy that makes the central idea
intuitive.


1. The comoving-frame transfer equation and the velocity-coupling term

In the comoving frame the radiative transfer equation along a ray of cosine
mu contains an extra term that couples adjacent frequencies, because a
photon's comoving frequency shifts as it propagates through the moving
medium.  Schematically,

    dI_nu/ds = eta_nu - chi_nu * I_nu  +  velocity-coupling terms,

where eta_nu is the emissivity, chi_nu the opacity, and s the path length.
In the non-relativistic, large-velocity-gradient limit the coupling term is
characterised by the Sobolev frequency scale

    Gamma = (V/R) * (1 + sigma * mu^2),

with units of s^-1, where V is the velocity, R the radius, mu the ray
cosine, and sigma = dln V/dln R - 1 the velocity-gradient parameter.
Gamma measures how fast a photon's comoving frequency shifts per unit path
length.  In a monotonic outflow (sigma > -1) Gamma > 0 for all rays:
photons redshift as they travel outward, so each frequency couples to the
next-lower frequency.  In a compression zone (sigma < -1) Gamma can become
negative for near-radial rays (mu^2 large): photons blueshift as they move
into the compression, so each frequency couples to the next-HIGHER
frequency.  The sign of Gamma - not of sigma alone - is what determines the
physical direction of frequency coupling, because Gamma depends on both
sigma and the ray angle mu.


2. Why the standard solver fails

CMFGEN's standard formal solver (the monotonic path, FG_J_CMF_V13) absorbs
the velocity coupling into a generalised opacity,

    chi_hat = chi + Gamma / dlog(nu),

where dlog(nu) is the logarithmic frequency-grid spacing.  The transfer
equation then reduces to a standard short-characteristics problem,

    dI/d(tau_hat) = I - S',   tau_hat = chi_hat * dz,
    S' = S + Q * I_upwind,    Q = (Gamma/dlog(nu)) / chi_hat,

with S = eta/chi_hat and I_upwind the intensity at the upwind (already
computed) frequency.  In monotonic flow Gamma > 0, so chi_hat > chi > 0 and
the formal solution is well behaved: optical depths are positive, intensities
attenuate as exp(-tau_hat), and the decreasing-frequency sweep always finds
I_upwind = I_PREV already computed.

When Gamma < 0 (compression), two failures occur:

  (a) Negative generalised opacity.  If |Gamma|/dlog(nu) > chi then
      chi_hat < 0.  A negative opacity is unphysical: optical depths become
      negative, the formal-solution exponential exp(tau_1 - tau_2) grows
      instead of decaying, intensities blow up or go negative, and NaNs
      propagate through the ray integration and into the moment equations
      and the BA matrix.

  (b) Wrong upwind frequency.  The standard solver always takes I_upwind =
      I_PREV (the next-higher frequency, already visited in the
      decreasing-frequency sweep).  In a compression zone the physical
      coupling is to the next-LOWER frequency, so the standard solver asks
      the wrong neighbour and obtains a source function with the wrong sign
      of frequency coupling.

A protective infrastructure clamp (sigma >= -0.999, applied everywhere
except the non-monotonic solver) prevents outright crashes by forcing
Gamma > 0, but it does so by replacing the true velocity gradient in
compression zones with a flat -0.999 plateau.  This destroys the physics:
the formal solution no longer represents the actual reversing flow, lines
formed in the compression get wrong profiles, and the energy balance is
corrupted because the wrong J_nu feeds into the rate equations.


3. The Knop fix: separate opacity from direction

The central insight of Knop et al. (2009) is that resistance (opacity) and
direction (which neighbour to ask) are two different things, and the
standard solver had bundled them together.  Knop separates them:

    chi_hat = chi + |Gamma| / dlog(nu)        (always > 0)
    Q       = (Gamma / dlog(nu)) / chi_hat     (signed, can be negative)
    S       = eta / chi_hat
    S'      = S + Q * I_upwind                 (signed source)

The magnitude |Gamma| sets the opacity, so chi_hat is strictly positive
regardless of whether the flow is expanding or compressing: no negative
optical depths, no exponentially growing intensities.  The signed Gamma is
retained in the dimensionless coupling coefficient Q, which satisfies
|Q| <= 1 always (because |Gamma|/dlog(nu) is part of chi_hat).  The sign of
Q carries the physical direction of frequency coupling: Q > 0 means
expanding flow (redshift coupling to the previous frequency), Q < 0 means
compressing flow (blueshift coupling to the next frequency).  The transfer
equation keeps the same short-characteristics form,

    dI/d(tau_hat) = I - S',   tau_hat = chi_hat * dz,

which is always well defined.  The physics of the compression is now
captured by the sign of Q and the choice of upwind intensity, not by a
negative opacity.


4. Sign-dependent upwind frequency

The critical innovation is the sign-dependent choice of upwind frequency.
The solver selects I_upwind based on the sign of Gamma (equivalently Q),
not on sigma alone:

    I_upwind = I_PREV   if Gamma >= 0   (expanding: standard path)
    I_upwind = I_NEXT   if Gamma <  0   (compressing: Knop fix)

The criterion is based on the ray-dependent Gamma = (V/R)(1 + sigma*mu^2),
not on sigma alone, because a depth with sigma < -1 may still have
Gamma > 0 for tangential rays (small mu^2).  Using Gamma ensures that each
ray asks the correct neighbour for its specific angle.


5. Gauss-Seidel coupling across great iterations

There is a catch.  CMFGEN sweeps frequencies in DECREASING order (high
frequency to low frequency).  At frequency nu_i the next-lower frequency
nu_{i+1} has NOT yet been computed in the current sweep, so I_NEXT is not
available directly.  The Knop solution is a Gauss-Seidel scheme across
great iterations:

  - On the FIRST great iteration, I_NEXT = 0 (cold start).  Compressing
    points get S' = S (no frequency coupling) - a crude but stable
    approximation that does not blow up because chi_hat is still positive.

  - After each great iteration, the computed intensities at every
    frequency, depth, and ray are STORED in the I_NEXT arrays.

  - On the NEXT great iteration, those stored values serve as the
    Gauss-Seidel approximation for I_NEXT in compression zones.

  - Over successive great iterations, I_NEXT converges to the correct
    value, and the frequency coupling in compression zones becomes
    physically accurate.

This is directly analogous to how Lambda iteration converges: each
iteration uses the previous iteration's radiation field, refining it until
convergence.  The convergence rate is governed by the spectral radius of
the iteration matrix; for |Q| ~ 1 (extreme compression) the spectral radius
is close to 1 and convergence is slow - typically one order of magnitude in
the maximum correction per one to two great iterations.  A warm restart
from a monotonic model of the same snapshot substantially reduces the
number of cold-start iterations needed.


6. Bypassing the moment equations and the dual-sigma architecture

The 0th and 1st moments of the transfer equation contain terms proportional
to (1 + sigma) that become large and negative when sigma < -1.  The
tridiagonal moment solver assumes diagonal dominance, which is lost when
these terms change sign, so the moment equations are unstable in
compression zones.  The non-monotonic solver therefore BYPASSES them
entirely: with USE_LAM_ES = T, the mean intensity J_nu is set directly to
the ray-based value TC from the formal solution, and the Eddington factors
(K/J, N/J) are computed from the ray moments via GET_MOMS_NON_REL rather
than from the moment solve.  The Eddington factors are still physically
correct - they come from the true formal solution - they simply bypass the
unstable tridiagonal solve.

To preserve compatibility with the rest of CMFGEN's infrastructure (which
was built for monotonic flow), the solver maintains TWO sigma arrays:

    SIGMA_TRUE : the unclamped dln V/dln R - 1, seen ONLY by the
                 non-monotonic formal solver.
    SIGMA      : clamped to >= -0.999, seen by every other routine
                 (moment equations, BA matrix, Ng acceleration, Sobolev
                 escape probabilities, grey opacity, regridding).

This separation ensures that the formal solution sees the true non-monotonic
physics while the infrastructure routines remain stable.  Existing monotonic
models are completely unaffected: if no depth has sigma_true < -1, the
non-monotonic solver behaves identically to the standard monotonic path, the
large I_NEXT arrays are not allocated, and the Gauss-Seidel machinery is
dormant.


7. Intensity clamping and NaN safety

Even with a strictly positive opacity, the explicit source term Q * I_NEXT
with Q < 0 can drive the intensity slightly negative in compression zones
on early iterations, when I_NEXT is still far from converged.  The solver
clamps ray intensities to a floor of 1e-30 whenever they are negative or
NaN, using the IEEE-754 self-comparison trick (x .ne. x is true only for
NaN, because Fortran's x < 0 test returns FALSE for NaN).  Five clamping
points guard the propagation chain: ray intensities (I_M, I_P), the stored
mean intensity JNU_STORE (preventing division by zero in f_Edd = K/J), the
I_NEXT storage arrays (preventing NaN contaminating the next great
iteration), the TC/FEDD arrays in COMP_J_BLANK, and the K/J division in
GET_MOMS_NON_REL.  All diagnostic counters use OpenMP ATOMIC operations to
avoid race conditions in the parallel ray loop.


8. Sparse storage for the Gauss-Seidel arrays

Storing intensities at all depths, all rays, and all frequencies for the
I_NEXT arrays would require N_D * N_P * N_CF * 8 bytes per array - tens of
GB for realistic grids.  The optimisation exploits the fact that only depths
with sigma_true < -1 can ever have Gamma < 0 (for any ray angle).  These
"compressing" depths are typically a small fraction of the grid.  The
solver counts N_comp, builds a sparse index mapping the full-depth index to
a compact 1...N_comp, and allocates I_P_DEPTH_NEXT and I_M_DEPTH_NEXT with
dimensions (N_comp, N_P, N_CF) - typically orders of magnitude smaller than
the full (N_D, N_P, N_CF) arrays.  If there are no compressing depths the
arrays are not allocated at all and the solver runs the standard monotonic
path.


An analogy: the reversing river

Before the mathematics, an analogy captures the whole idea.

The setup.  Imagine a river flowing through a series of towns.  The river
is the frequency axis: each town sits at one frequency.  Leaves floating on
the river are photons.  The current is the velocity coupling Gamma: it
carries leaves from one town to the next.  The muddiness of the water is
the generalised opacity chi_hat: it determines how far a leaf can travel
before it sinks (is absorbed) or is replaced by a new one (is emitted).

Normal flow (monotonic expansion).  In a normal river the current always
flows DOWNSTREAM - from high frequency to low frequency (redshift).  Each
town receives its leaves from the town UPSTREAM (the next-higher frequency,
which was already visited in the sweep).  The muddiness is always positive
(the current adds to it), so leaves travel a finite distance and everything
is well behaved.  This is the standard CMFGEN solver.

The problem: a reversing river.  Now imagine the river hits a gorge and the
current REVERSES in some stretches - the water flows upstream there.  This
is a compression zone (Gamma < 0).  Two things go wrong:

  (a) Negative muddiness.  The old formula ADDED the current to the
      muddiness.  When the current reverses, it SUBTRACTS, and the
      muddiness can go negative.  A river with negative muddiness is
      nonsensical - leaves would GROW stronger as they travel,
      exponentially.  In the code this means exp(+tau_hat) instead of
      exp(-tau_hat): intensities blow up or go negative, and NaNs
      propagate.

  (b) Wrong upstream town.  In the reversed stretch, leaves now arrive
      from DOWNSTREAM (the next-LOWER frequency), not from upstream.  But
      the standard solver always asks the upstream town for its leaves - so
      it asks the wrong neighbour and gets the wrong answer.

The Knop fix, in river terms.  Knop et al. (2009) make two changes:

  (a) Muddiness is always positive.  Use the STRENGTH of the current (its
      absolute value |Gamma|) to set the muddiness, never its signed value.
      The river is always muddy - leaves always attenuate.  The muddiness
      no longer cares which way the current flows.

  (b) Ask the right neighbour.  Keep the SIGNED current in a separate
      quantity (the source-function correction Q).  When Q > 0 (flowing
      downstream), ask the upstream town (the previous frequency, already
      computed).  When Q < 0 (flowing upstream), ask the DOWNSTREAM town
      (the next frequency).  The sign of Q tells each town which neighbour
      to listen to.

The catch: the downstream town hasn't spoken yet.  CMFGEN visits towns in
DOWNSTREAM order (high frequency to low frequency).  In a reversed stretch,
the town you need to ask (downstream, lower frequency) hasn't been visited
YET in this round.  So on the first pass you pretend it said nothing
(I_NEXT = 0) - a crude but stable guess.  After each complete round (a
"great iteration"), you REMEMBER what every town said and use that memory
next time.  Over several rounds the remembered answers converge to the
truth.  This is the Gauss-Seidel scheme across great iterations.

The dual-current bookkeeping.  To avoid confusing the rest of the CMFGEN
machinery (which was built for a one-way river), the solver keeps TWO
current maps: SIGMA_TRUE (the real, reversing current - seen only by the
new solver) and SIGMA (a fake, always-downstream current clamped to
>= -0.999, seen by everything else).  The old machinery keeps working as if
the river never reverses; only the new formal solver knows the truth.

Summary of the analogy:

    River (towns along it)            frequency grid
    Leaves on the water               photons (I_nu)
    Current                           velocity coupling Gamma
    Muddiness of the water            generalised opacity chi_hat
    Where leaves come from            source function S'
    Upstream town                     previous frequency (I_PREV)
    Downstream town                   next frequency (I_NEXT)
    Reversed stretch                  compression zone (Gamma < 0)
    One complete round of visits      one great iteration
    Remembering last round's answers  Gauss-Seidel coupling
    Two current maps                  SIGMA_TRUE vs SIGMA

The key insight is that RESISTANCE (opacity) and DIRECTION (which neighbour
to ask) are two different things.  The standard solver bundled them
together; Knop et al. separated them.


Caveats and limitations

The Knop et al. (2009) solver makes the formal solution stable and
physically meaningful in compression zones, but it is not a complete
non-monotonic radiative-transfer code.  Three limitations should be noted:

  (a) Single-resonance approximation.  The solver operates within CMFGEN's
      comoving-frame framework, which inherits the Sobolev
      large-velocity-gradient picture: each frequency is assumed to couple
      to ONE upwind frequency at each depth.  In a genuine velocity
      reversal a single observer-frame frequency can resonate with MULTIPLE
      comoving frequencies along a single ray (multi-root resonance).  The
      solver picks a single upwind frequency based on the local sign of
      Gamma; it does not trace all resonance points.  Lines formed deep
      inside a strongly reversing flow can therefore have wrong profiles
      even though J_nu stays finite.  The solver is reliable for shocks and
      decelerating shells where the reversal is mild or where the line
      forms outside the reversal; it is unreliable for lines that form
      inside a deep velocity minimum with multiple resonance points.

  (b) Approximate BA Jacobian in compression zones.  The BA variation
      matrix (the approximate-Newton correction to the populations) uses
      the CLAMPED sigma >= -0.999, not SIGMA_TRUE, because the true
      sigma < -1 would make the BA matrix singular (the (1 + sigma) terms
      change sign).  The Eddington factors fed into the BA operator ARE
      computed from the true formal solution, so the radiation-field
      geometry is correct, but the velocity-coupling part of the Jacobian
      is approximate in compression zones.  With the depth-independent
      (DIAG, NUM_BNDS = 1) solver this is stable but may converge more
      slowly; with the tridiagonal (TRIDIAG, NUM_BNDS = 3) solver the
      cross-depth coupling can make the BA matrix singular for strongly
      non-monotonic flows, which is why the production configuration uses
      DIAG.  The iteration strategy (alternating Lambda, Full, and Ng
      iterations) compensates for the approximate Jacobian.

  (c) Slow Gauss-Seidel convergence on cold start.  Because I_NEXT = 0 on
      the first great iteration, compression zones begin with no frequency
      coupling.  The coupling is filled in only over successive great
      iterations, so the first iteration of a non-monotonic model is a
      cruder approximation than a monotonic model's first iteration.  A
      warm restart from a monotonic model of the same snapshot, or the use
      of LTE initial populations (LTE_EST = T) to stabilise the early
      population solver, substantially reduces the number of cold-start
      iterations needed.  In our SN_B models the combination of LTE_EST = T
      and the Knop solver converges in roughly 10-20 great iterations,
      with the maximum population correction decreasing by approximately
      one order of magnitude per one to two iterations.
